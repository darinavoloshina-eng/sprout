In Waco, TX, the last spring frost usually comes around February 13 and the first fall frost around November 25, giving you about 285 frost-free days. That's a long season with room for two gardens: cool-season crops in spring, warm-season crops through summer, and a second planting of greens and roots for fall.

Your first seeds go in indoors around November 28 (onions), peppers follow around December 19 and tomatoes go outside around February 20, once the soil has warmed. Garlic goes in around November 25 for next summer's harvest.

Common questions

When is the last frost in Waco, TX?

Around February 13 on average, based on the last ten years of daily temperature records. Any given year can run a week or two either way, so keep an eye on the forecast before planting frost-tender crops.

When is the first frost in Waco, TX?

Around November 25 on average. Cover or harvest tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil before then.

When should I plant tomatoes in Waco, TX?

Start tomato seeds indoors around January 2 and plant them outside around February 20, after the last frost, when nights stay above about 50°F.

Can I grow a fall garden in Waco, TX?

Yes. Cool-season crops do well planted in late summer and fall: cauliflower around August 26, broccoli around September 2, arugula around September 9 and cabbage around September 9.

How these dates are worked out: the last and first frost are averages over the last ten years of the days the temperature near Waco dropped to 34°F or below, from Open-Meteo's historical weather records. We count 34°F rather than 32°F because frost forms in the coldest spots of a garden before the wider area reads freezing.
