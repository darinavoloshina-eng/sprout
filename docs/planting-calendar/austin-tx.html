When to Plant Vegetables in Austin, TX

Seed-starting and planting dates for 30 vegetables, herbs and fruits, worked out from Austin's real frost history.

Frost-free days289

In Austin, TX, the last spring frost usually comes around February 25 and the first fall frost around December 11, giving you about 289 frost-free days. That's a long season with room for two gardens: cool-season crops in spring, warm-season crops through summer, and a second planting of greens and roots for fall.

Your first seeds go in indoors around December 10 (onions), peppers follow around December 31 and tomatoes go outside around March 4, once the soil has warmed. Garlic goes in around November 25 for next summer's harvest.

Common questions

When is the last frost in Austin, TX?

Around February 25 on average, based on the last ten years of daily temperature records. Any given year can run a week or two either way, so keep an eye on the forecast before planting frost-tender crops.

When is the first frost in Austin, TX?

Around December 11 on average. Cover or harvest tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil before then.

When should I plant tomatoes in Austin, TX?

Start tomato seeds indoors around January 14 and plant them outside around March 4, after the last frost, when nights stay above about 50°F.

Can I grow a fall garden in Austin, TX?

Yes. Cool-season crops do well planted in late summer and fall: cauliflower around September 11, broccoli around September 18, arugula around September 25 and cabbage around September 25.

How these dates are worked out: the last and first frost are averages over the last ten years of the days the temperature near Austin dropped to 34°F or below, from Open-Meteo's historical weather records. We count 34°F rather than 32°F because frost forms in the coldest spots of a garden before the wider area reads freezing. Each crop's dates count forward or back from those frosts, the same way the GardenWise app does. New to starting seeds? Read When to Start Seeds Indoors.
