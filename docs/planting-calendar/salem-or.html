In Salem, OR, the last spring frost usually comes around April 5 and the first fall frost around November 9, giving you about 218 frost-free days. That's a long season with room for two gardens: cool-season crops in spring, warm-season crops through summer, and a second planting of greens and roots for fall.

Your first seeds go in indoors around January 18 (onions), peppers follow around March 20 and tomatoes go outside around May 29, once the soil has warmed. Garlic goes in around November 25 for next summer's harvest.

Common questions

When is the last frost in Salem, OR?

Around April 5 on average, based on the last ten years of daily temperature records. Any given year can run a week or two either way, so keep an eye on the forecast before planting frost-tender crops.

When is the first frost in Salem, OR?

Around November 9 on average. Cover or harvest tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil before then.

When should I plant tomatoes in Salem, OR?

Start tomato seeds indoors around April 10 and plant them outside around May 29, after the last frost, when nights stay above about 50°F.

Can I grow a fall garden in Salem, OR?

Yes. Cool-season crops do well planted in late summer and fall: cauliflower around August 10, broccoli around August 17, arugula around August 24 and cabbage around August 24.

How these dates are worked out: the last and first frost are averages over the last ten years of the days the temperature near Salem dropped to 34°F or below, from Open-Meteo's historical weather records. We count 34°F rather than 32°F because frost forms in the coldest spots of a garden before the wider area reads freezing. Each crop's dates count forward or back from those frosts, the same way the GardenWise app does. New to starting seeds? Read When to Start Seeds Indoors.
