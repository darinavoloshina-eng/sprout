In Birmingham, AL, the last spring frost usually comes around March 18 and the first fall frost around November 17, giving you about 244 frost-free days. That's a long season with room for two gardens: cool-season crops in spring, warm-season crops through summer, and a second planting of greens and roots for fall.

Your first seeds go in indoors around December 31 (onions), peppers follow around January 21 and tomatoes go outside around March 25, once the soil has warmed. Garlic goes in around November 25 for next summer's harvest.

Common questions

When is the last frost in Birmingham, AL?

Around March 18 on average, based on the last ten years of daily temperature records. Any given year can run a week or two either way, so keep an eye on the forecast before planting frost-tender crops.

When is the first frost in Birmingham, AL?

Around November 17 on average. Cover or harvest tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil before then.

When should I plant tomatoes in Birmingham, AL?

Start tomato seeds indoors around February 4 and plant them outside around March 25, after the last frost, when nights stay above about 50°F.

Can I grow a fall garden in Birmingham, AL?

Yes. Cool-season crops do well planted in late summer and fall: cauliflower around August 18, broccoli around August 25, arugula around September 1 and cabbage around September 1.

How these dates are worked out: the last and first frost are averages over the last ten years of the days the temperature near Birmingham dropped to 34°F or below, from Open-Meteo's historical weather records. We count 34°F rather than 32°F because frost forms in the coldest spots of a garden before the wider area reads freezing. Each crop's dates count forward or back from those frosts, the same way the GardenWise app does. New to starting seeds? Read When to Start Seeds Indoors.
