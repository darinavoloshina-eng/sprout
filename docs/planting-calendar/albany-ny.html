When to Plant Vegetables in Albany, NY

Seed-starting and planting dates for 30 vegetables, herbs and fruits, worked out from Albany's real frost history.

Frost-free days187

In Albany, NY, the last spring frost usually comes around April 26 and the first fall frost around October 30, giving you about 187 frost-free days. That's enough for a full summer of tomatoes, squash and beans, plus a fall round of cool-season crops if you sow them by late summer.

Your first seeds go in indoors around February 8 (onions), peppers follow around March 1 and tomatoes go outside around May 3, once the soil has warmed. Garlic goes in around November 20 for next summer's harvest.

Common questions

When is the last frost in Albany, NY?

Around April 26 on average, based on the last ten years of daily temperature records. Any given year can run a week or two either way, so keep an eye on the forecast before planting frost-tender crops.

When is the first frost in Albany, NY?

Around October 30 on average. Cover or harvest tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil before then.

When should I plant tomatoes in Albany, NY?

Start tomato seeds indoors around March 15 and plant them outside around May 3, after the last frost, when nights stay above about 50°F.

Can I grow a fall garden in Albany, NY?

Yes. Cool-season crops do well planted in late summer and fall: cauliflower around July 31, broccoli around August 7, arugula around August 14 and cabbage around August 14.

How these dates are worked out: the last and first frost are averages over the last ten years of the days the temperature near Albany dropped to 34°F or below, from Open-Meteo's historical weather records. We count 34°F rather than 32°F because frost forms in the coldest spots of a garden before the wider area reads freezing.
