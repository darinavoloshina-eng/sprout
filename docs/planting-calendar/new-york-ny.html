Frost-free days214

In New York, NY, the last spring frost usually comes around April 13 and the first fall frost around November 13, giving you about 214 frost-free days. That's a long season with room for two gardens: cool-season crops in spring, warm-season crops through summer, and a second planting of greens and roots for fall.

Your first seeds go in indoors around January 26 (onions), peppers follow around February 16 and tomatoes go outside around April 20, once the soil has warmed. Garlic goes in around November 25 for next summer's harvest.

Common questions

When is the last frost in New York, NY?

Around April 13 on average, based on the last ten years of daily temperature records. Any given year can run a week or two either way, so keep an eye on the forecast before planting frost-tender crops.

When is the first frost in New York, NY?

Around November 13 on average. Cover or harvest tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil before then.

When should I plant tomatoes in New York, NY?

Start tomato seeds indoors around March 2 and plant them outside around April 20, after the last frost, when nights stay above about 50°F.

Can I grow a fall garden in New York, NY?

Yes. Cool-season crops do well planted in late summer and fall: cauliflower around August 14, broccoli around August 21, arugula around August 28 and cabbage around August 28.

How these dates are worked out: the last and first frost are averages over the last ten years of the days the temperature near New York dropped to 34°F or below, from Open-Meteo's historical weather records. We count 34°F rather than 32°F because frost forms in the coldest spots of a garden before the wider area reads freezing.
