Seed-starting and planting dates for 30 vegetables, herbs and fruits, worked out from Portland's real frost history.

Frost-free days194

In Portland, ME, the last spring frost usually comes around April 23 and the first fall frost around November 3, giving you about 194 frost-free days. That's enough for a full summer of tomatoes, squash and beans, plus a fall round of cool-season crops if you sow them by late summer.

Your first seeds go in indoors around February 5 (onions), peppers follow around February 26 and tomatoes go outside around April 30, once the soil has warmed. Garlic goes in around November 24 for next summer's harvest.

Common questions

When is the last frost in Portland, ME?

Around April 23 on average, based on the last ten years of daily temperature records. Any given year can run a week or two either way, so keep an eye on the forecast before planting frost-tender crops.

When is the first frost in Portland, ME?

Around November 3 on average. Cover or harvest tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil before then.

When should I plant tomatoes in Portland, ME?

Start tomato seeds indoors around March 12 and plant them outside around April 30, after the last frost, when nights stay above about 50°F.

Can I grow a fall garden in Portland, ME?

Yes. Cool-season crops do well planted in late summer and fall: cauliflower around August 4, broccoli around August 11, arugula around August 18 and cabbage around August 18.

How these dates are worked out: the last and first frost are averages over the last ten years of the days the temperature near Portland dropped to 34°F or below, from Open-Meteo's historical weather records. We count 34°F rather than 32°F because frost forms in the coldest spots of a garden before the wider area reads freezing. Each crop's dates count forward or back from those frosts, the same way the GardenWise app does. New to starting seeds? Read When to Start Seeds Indoors.
