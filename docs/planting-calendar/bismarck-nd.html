In Bismarck, ND, the last spring frost usually comes around May 6 and the first fall frost around October 8, giving you about 155 frost-free days. That's enough for a full summer of tomatoes, squash and beans, plus a fall round of cool-season crops if you sow them by late summer.

Your first seeds go in indoors around February 18 (onions), peppers follow around March 17 and tomatoes go outside around May 26, once the soil has warmed. Garlic goes in around October 29 for next summer's harvest.

Common questions

When is the last frost in Bismarck, ND?

Around May 6 on average, based on the last ten years of daily temperature records. Any given year can run a week or two either way, so keep an eye on the forecast before planting frost-tender crops.

When is the first frost in Bismarck, ND?

Around October 8 on average. Cover or harvest tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil before then.

When should I plant tomatoes in Bismarck, ND?

Start tomato seeds indoors around April 7 and plant them outside around May 26, after the last frost, when nights stay above about 50°F.

Can I grow a fall garden in Bismarck, ND?

Yes. Cool-season crops do well planted in late summer and fall: cauliflower around July 9, broccoli around July 16, arugula around July 23 and cabbage around July 23.

How these dates are worked out: the last and first frost are averages over the last ten years of the days the temperature near Bismarck dropped to 34°F or below, from Open-Meteo's historical weather records. We count 34°F rather than 32°F because frost forms in the coldest spots of a garden before the wider area reads freezing.
