In Concord, NH, the last spring frost usually comes around April 28 and the first fall frost around October 23, giving you about 178 frost-free days. That's enough for a full summer of tomatoes, squash and beans, plus a fall round of cool-season crops if you sow them by late summer.

Your first seeds go in indoors around February 10 (onions), peppers follow around March 10 and tomatoes go outside around May 19, once the soil has warmed. Garlic goes in around November 13 for next summer's harvest.

Common questions

When is the last frost in Concord, NH?

Around April 28 on average, based on the last ten years of daily temperature records. Any given year can run a week or two either way, so keep an eye on the forecast before planting frost-tender crops.

When is the first frost in Concord, NH?

Around October 23 on average. Cover or harvest tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil before then.

When should I plant tomatoes in Concord, NH?

Start tomato seeds indoors around March 31 and plant them outside around May 19, after the last frost, when nights stay above about 50°F.

Can I grow a fall garden in Concord, NH?

Yes. Cool-season crops do well planted in late summer and fall: cauliflower around July 24, broccoli around July 31, arugula around August 7 and cabbage around August 7.

How these dates are worked out: the last and first frost are averages over the last ten years of the days the temperature near Concord dropped to 34°F or below, from Open-Meteo's historical weather records. We count 34°F rather than 32°F because frost forms in the coldest spots of a garden before the wider area reads freezing. Each crop's dates count forward or back from those frosts, the same way the GardenWise app does. New to starting seeds? Read When to Start Seeds Indoors.
