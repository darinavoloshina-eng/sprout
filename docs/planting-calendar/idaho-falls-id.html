In Idaho Falls, ID, the last spring frost usually comes around May 15 and the first fall frost around October 3, giving you about 141 frost-free days. That's enough for a full summer of tomatoes, squash and beans, plus a fall round of cool-season crops if you sow them by late summer.

Your first seeds go in indoors around February 27 (onions), peppers follow around March 23 and tomatoes go outside around June 1, once the soil has warmed. Garlic goes in around October 24 for next summer's harvest.

Common questions

When is the last frost in Idaho Falls, ID?

Around May 15 on average, based on the last ten years of daily temperature records. Any given year can run a week or two either way, so keep an eye on the forecast before planting frost-tender crops.

When is the first frost in Idaho Falls, ID?

Around October 3 on average. Cover or harvest tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil before then.

When should I plant tomatoes in Idaho Falls, ID?

Start tomato seeds indoors around April 13 and plant them outside around June 1, after the last frost, when nights stay above about 50°F.

Can I grow a fall garden in Idaho Falls, ID?

Yes. Cool-season crops do well planted in late summer and fall: cauliflower around July 4, broccoli around July 11, arugula around July 18 and cabbage around July 18.

How these dates are worked out: the last and first frost are averages over the last ten years of the days the temperature near Idaho Falls dropped to 34°F or below, from Open-Meteo's historical weather records. We count 34°F rather than 32°F because frost forms in the coldest spots of a garden before the wider area reads freezing.
