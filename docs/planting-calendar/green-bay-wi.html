In Green Bay, WI, the last spring frost usually comes around May 4 and the first fall frost around October 25, giving you about 174 frost-free days. That's enough for a full summer of tomatoes, squash and beans, plus a fall round of cool-season crops if you sow them by late summer.

Your first seeds go in indoors around February 16 (onions), peppers follow around March 9 and tomatoes go outside around May 11, once the soil has warmed. Garlic goes in around November 15 for next summer's harvest.

Common questions

When is the last frost in Green Bay, WI?

Around May 4 on average, based on the last ten years of daily temperature records. Any given year can run a week or two either way, so keep an eye on the forecast before planting frost-tender crops.

When is the first frost in Green Bay, WI?

Around October 25 on average. Cover or harvest tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil before then.

When should I plant tomatoes in Green Bay, WI?

Start tomato seeds indoors around March 23 and plant them outside around May 11, after the last frost, when nights stay above about 50°F.

Can I grow a fall garden in Green Bay, WI?

Yes. Cool-season crops do well planted in late summer and fall: cauliflower around July 26, broccoli around August 2, arugula around August 9 and cabbage around August 9.

How these dates are worked out: the last and first frost are averages over the last ten years of the days the temperature near Green Bay dropped to 34°F or below, from Open-Meteo's historical weather records. We count 34°F rather than 32°F because frost forms in the coldest spots of a garden before the wider area reads freezing. Each crop's dates count forward or back from those frosts, the same way the GardenWise app does. New to starting seeds? Read When to Start Seeds Indoors.
