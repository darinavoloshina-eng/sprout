In Gainesville, FL, the last spring frost usually comes around January 28 and the first fall frost around December 11, giving you about 317 frost-free days. That's a long season with room for two gardens: cool-season crops in spring, warm-season crops through summer, and a second planting of greens and roots for fall.

Your first seeds go in indoors around November 12 (onions), peppers follow around December 3 and tomatoes go outside around February 4, once the soil has warmed. Garlic goes in around November 25 for next summer's harvest.

Common questions

When is the last frost in Gainesville, FL?

Around January 28 on average, based on the last ten years of daily temperature records. Any given year can run a week or two either way, so keep an eye on the forecast before planting frost-tender crops.

When is the first frost in Gainesville, FL?

Around December 11 on average. Cover or harvest tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil before then.

When should I plant tomatoes in Gainesville, FL?

Start tomato seeds indoors around December 17 and plant them outside around February 4, after the last frost, when nights stay above about 50°F.

Can I grow a fall garden in Gainesville, FL?

Yes. Cool-season crops do well planted in late summer and fall: cauliflower around September 11, broccoli around September 18, arugula around September 25 and cabbage around September 25.

How these dates are worked out: the last and first frost are averages over the last ten years of the days the temperature near Gainesville dropped to 34°F or below, from Open-Meteo's historical weather records. We count 34°F rather than 32°F because frost forms in the coldest spots of a garden before the wider area reads freezing.
