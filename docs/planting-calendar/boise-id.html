In Boise, ID, the last spring frost usually comes around April 24 and the first fall frost around October 21, giving you about 180 frost-free days. That's enough for a full summer of tomatoes, squash and beans, plus a fall round of cool-season crops if you sow them by late summer.

Your first seeds go in indoors around February 6 (onions), peppers follow around February 27 and tomatoes go outside around May 1, once the soil has warmed. Garlic goes in around November 11 for next summer's harvest.

Common questions

When is the last frost in Boise, ID?

Around April 24 on average, based on the last ten years of daily temperature records. Any given year can run a week or two either way, so keep an eye on the forecast before planting frost-tender crops.

When is the first frost in Boise, ID?

Around October 21 on average. Cover or harvest tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil before then.

When should I plant tomatoes in Boise, ID?

Start tomato seeds indoors around March 13 and plant them outside around May 1, after the last frost, when nights stay above about 50°F.

Can I grow a fall garden in Boise, ID?

Yes. Cool-season crops do well planted in late summer and fall: cauliflower around July 22, broccoli around July 29, arugula around August 5 and cabbage around August 5.

How these dates are worked out: the last and first frost are averages over the last ten years of the days the temperature near Boise dropped to 34°F or below, from Open-Meteo's historical weather records. We count 34°F rather than 32°F because frost forms in the coldest spots of a garden before the wider area reads freezing.
