In Chicago, IL, the last spring frost usually comes around April 19 and the first fall frost around November 6, giving you about 201 frost-free days. That's a long season with room for two gardens: cool-season crops in spring, warm-season crops through summer, and a second planting of greens and roots for fall.

Your first seeds go in indoors around February 1 (onions), peppers follow around February 22 and tomatoes go outside around April 26, once the soil has warmed. Garlic goes in around November 25 for next summer's harvest.

Common questions

When is the last frost in Chicago, IL?

Around April 19 on average, based on the last ten years of daily temperature records. Any given year can run a week or two either way, so keep an eye on the forecast before planting frost-tender crops.

When is the first frost in Chicago, IL?

Around November 6 on average. Cover or harvest tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil before then.

When should I plant tomatoes in Chicago, IL?

Start tomato seeds indoors around March 8 and plant them outside around April 26, after the last frost, when nights stay above about 50°F.

Can I grow a fall garden in Chicago, IL?

Yes. Cool-season crops do well planted in late summer and fall: cauliflower around August 7, broccoli around August 14, arugula around August 21 and cabbage around August 21.

How these dates are worked out: the last and first frost are averages over the last ten years of the days the temperature near Chicago dropped to 34°F or below, from Open-Meteo's historical weather records. We count 34°F rather than 32°F because frost forms in the coldest spots of a garden before the wider area reads freezing. Each crop's dates count forward or back from those frosts, the same way the GardenWise app does. New to starting seeds? Read When to Start Seeds Indoors.
