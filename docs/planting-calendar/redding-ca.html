In Redding, CA, the last spring frost usually comes around March 5 and the first fall frost around December 16, giving you about 286 frost-free days. That's a long season with room for two gardens: cool-season crops in spring, warm-season crops through summer, and a second planting of greens and roots for fall.

Your first seeds go in indoors around December 18 (onions), peppers follow around February 5 and tomatoes go outside around April 16, once the soil has warmed. Garlic goes in around November 25 for next summer's harvest.

Common questions

When is the last frost in Redding, CA?

Around March 5 on average, based on the last ten years of daily temperature records. Any given year can run a week or two either way, so keep an eye on the forecast before planting frost-tender crops.

When is the first frost in Redding, CA?

Around December 16 on average. Cover or harvest tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil before then.

When should I plant tomatoes in Redding, CA?

Start tomato seeds indoors around February 26 and plant them outside around April 16, after the last frost, when nights stay above about 50°F.

Can I grow a fall garden in Redding, CA?

Yes. Cool-season crops do well planted in late summer and fall: cauliflower around September 16, broccoli around September 23, arugula around September 30 and cabbage around September 30.

How these dates are worked out: the last and first frost are averages over the last ten years of the days the temperature near Redding dropped to 34°F or below, from Open-Meteo's historical weather records. We count 34°F rather than 32°F because frost forms in the coldest spots of a garden before the wider area reads freezing.
